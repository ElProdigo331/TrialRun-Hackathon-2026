3. Industry-Standard Features (SPE Literature)

These features are derived from established petroleum engineering formulas published in peer-reviewed SPE (Society of Petroleum Engineers) journals and widely used in reservoir characterization.

3.1 Reservoir Quality Index (RQI)

RQI = 0.0314 × √(k / φ)

Description: The Reservoir Quality Index quantifies the quality of the reservoir rock by relating permeability to porosity. It represents the average hydraulic radius of pore throats and directly indicates the rock's ability to transmit fluids.

Interpretation: Higher RQI values indicate better reservoir quality with larger, more connected pore throats that facilitate fluid flow. Wells with high RQI typically exhibit higher production rates.

Units: Micrometers (μm)

Source: Amaefule, J.O., Altunbay, M., Tiab, D., Kersey, D.G., and Keelan, D.K. (1993). "Enhanced Reservoir Description: Using Core and Log Data to Identify Hydraulic (Flow) Units and Predict Permeability in Uncored Intervals/Wells." SPE Paper 26436, presented at the 68th Annual Technical Conference and Exhibition, Houston, Texas.

3.2 Flow Zone Indicator (FZI)

FZI = RQI / (φ / (1 - φ))

Description: The Flow Zone Indicator classifies reservoir rocks into distinct hydraulic flow units—zones with similar pore throat characteristics and flow behavior. It normalizes RQI by the ratio of pore volume to grain volume.

Interpretation: Rocks with similar FZI values belong to the same hydraulic flow unit and exhibit similar flow characteristics regardless of their absolute porosity or permeability. This enables better prediction of flow behavior in heterogeneous reservoirs.

Units: Micrometers (μm)

Source: Amaefule et al. (1993), SPE 26436. Also referenced in: Tiab, D. and Donaldson, E.C. (2015). "Petrophysics: Theory and Practice of Measuring Reservoir Rock and Fluid Transport Properties," 4th Edition, Gulf Professional Publishing.

3.3 Vp/Vs Ratio

Vp/Vs = Vp / Vs

Description: The ratio of compressional (P-wave) velocity to shear (S-wave) velocity is a fundamental rock physics parameter used for lithology discrimination and fluid identification.

Interpretation: In clastic reservoirs:

  • Clean sandstones typically have Vp/Vs ≈ 1.6-1.8
  • Shales typically have Vp/Vs > 2.0
  • Gas-saturated sands show lower Vp/Vs due to reduced P-wave velocity

Source: Castagna, J.P., Batzle, M.L., and Eastwood, R.L. (1985). "Relationships between compressional-wave and shear-wave velocities in clastic silicate rocks." Geophysics, 50(4), 571-581. Also: Mavko, G., Mukerji, T., and Dvorkin, J. (2020). "The Rock Physics Handbook," 3rd Edition, Cambridge University Press.
4. Derived Rock Quality Features

These features were engineered based on guidance from industry experts at the hackathon who emphasized that "good rock will more than likely have oil."

"Good rock will more than likely have oil. Focus on rock quality indicators."
— Industry Expert Panel, Energy AI Hackathon 2026

4.1 Phi-Perm Product

phi_perm_product = φ × log(1 + k)

Description: Combines porosity and permeability into a single productivity indicator. The logarithm is applied to permeability to handle its typically log-normal distribution and wide range of values.

Rationale: Neither high porosity nor high permeability alone guarantees production. A rock needs both storage capacity (porosity) AND flow capability (permeability). This product captures their combined effect.

4.2 Rock Quality Index (Simplified)

rock_quality = φ / (GR + 1)

Description: A clean sand index that rewards high porosity and penalizes high gamma ray (shale content).

Rationale: In clastic reservoirs, the best producing intervals are clean sands with high porosity and low gamma ray. Shale laminations (high GR) reduce effective permeability even in porous rocks.

4.3 Impedance Ratio

impedance_ratio = AI / (SI + 1)

Description: The ratio of acoustic impedance to shear impedance provides lithology discrimination capability.

Rationale: Different rock types and fluid saturations produce distinct impedance signatures. This ratio helps identify favorable reservoir facies.

4.4 Net-to-Gross Ratio

net_to_gross = 1 - facies_5_pct - facies_6_pct

Description: Estimates the proportion of net pay (productive sand) versus gross interval (including non-productive shales). Facies 5 and 6 represent shale-prone lithologies.

Rationale: Wells with higher net-to-gross ratios have more pay zone and typically produce more oil. This is a standard reservoir engineering metric.

Source: Standard reservoir engineering practice. See: Dake, L.P. (1978). "Fundamentals of Reservoir Engineering," Elsevier.

4.5 Storage Capacity

storage_capacity = φ × depth_range

Description: Proxy for total pore volume available for hydrocarbon storage, combining average porosity with the thickness of the productive interval.

Rationale: A thin high-porosity zone may store less oil than a thicker moderate-porosity zone. This feature captures volumetric storage potential.

4.6 Flow Quality

flow_quality = log(1 + k) / (GR + 1)

Description: Permeability per unit of shaliness—how much flow capacity exists relative to shale content.

Rationale: Identifies zones where high permeability exists despite moderate shale content, or conversely, where low shale content doesn't translate to good flow properties.

5. Analog Well Similarity Features

These features implement the insight from Nataly (Hackathon Architect) to correlate preproduction wells with known good producers based on rock quality characteristics.

"Look for correlation of known wells in good sand/rock that historically produced oil to the training wells."
— Nataly, Hackathon Architect

5.1 Analog Similarity

analog_similarity = 1 / (1 + min_distance_to_good_producer)

Description: Measures similarity to the nearest high-producing well based on rock quality features (phi, perm, GR, RQI). "Good producers" are defined as wells in the top 25th percentile of production.

Rationale: Wells with rock properties similar to known good producers are more likely to produce well. This is a data-driven implementation of the analog well selection method used in industry.

Implementation: Euclidean distance is calculated in normalized feature space (phi_mean, perm_mean, GR_mean, RQI) to the nearest good producer.

Source: Analog well analysis is standard practice in prospect evaluation. See: Rose, P.R. (2001). "Risk Analysis and Management of Petroleum Exploration Ventures," AAPG Methods in Exploration Series No. 12.

5.2 Analog Production Proxy

analog_production_proxy = Σ(production_i × similarity_weight_i) / Σ(similarity_weight_i)

Description: Weighted average of production from the most similar good-producing wells. Similarity weights decay with distance in feature space.

Rationale: Instead of just identifying the nearest analog, this feature estimates expected production based on a weighted ensemble of similar producers, providing a more robust prediction.

6. Spatial Proximity Features

Analysis of the training data revealed a strong spatial pattern: wells on the left side of the map (low X coordinates) tend to produce significantly more oil, particularly in the bottom-left and top-left corners.

Data Observation: Correlation analysis showed that X coordinate has a negative correlation with production, and the 2D sand proportion map shows higher sand content in the left region of the field.

6.1 Proximity to High Producers

proximity_to_high_prod = 1 / (1 + distance_to_high_producer_centroid)

Description: Inverse distance to the spatial centroid of high-producing wells (top 25th percentile).

Rationale: High production often clusters spatially due to underlying geological features (depositional environment, structural traps). Proximity to known high producers indicates favorable location.

6.2 Left Region Score

left_region_score = 1 / (1 + X_coordinate)

Description: Simple proximity measure to the left edge of the field where production is empirically higher.

Rationale: Captures the observed spatial trend without overfitting to specific well locations. Acts as a regularized spatial feature.

6.3 Spatial Production Proxy

spatial_production_proxy = Σ(production_i / distance_i) / Σ(1 / distance_i)

Description: Inverse-distance weighted average of production from nearby wells.

Rationale: Implements spatial interpolation (kriging-like) to estimate expected production based on geographic neighbors, capturing local geological trends.

Source: Spatial interpolation methods in geostatistics. See: Isaaks, E.H. and Srivastava, R.M. (1989). "Applied Geostatistics," Oxford University Press.
7. Best Zone Features

Traditional aggregation (mean, std) of depth-level measurements loses important information about the heterogeneity within each well. These features preserve information about the best-quality rock zone—the "pay zone" that drives production.

Problem Addressed: A well might have excellent rock properties at one depth but poor properties elsewhere. Simple averaging obscures this. Production often comes primarily from the best zone, not the average zone.

7.1 Best Zone Porosity (best_zone_phi)

Description: Porosity at the depth with the highest rock quality score (phi/GR).

7.2 Best Zone Permeability (best_zone_perm)

Description: Permeability at the depth with the highest rock quality score.

7.3 Best Zone Gamma Ray (best_zone_GR)

Description: Gamma Ray at the depth with the highest rock quality score (expected to be low for clean sand).

7.4 Best Zone Quality

best_zone_quality = max(φ / (GR + 1)) across all depths

Description: The rock quality score at the best depth, representing the peak reservoir quality in the well.

7.5 Zone Quality Contrast

zone_quality_contrast = best_zone_phi - worst_zone_phi

Description: Difference in porosity between the best and worst zones, quantifying vertical heterogeneity.

Rationale: High contrast suggests layered reservoir with distinct pay zones. Low contrast suggests more homogeneous reservoir. This affects completion strategy and production behavior.
